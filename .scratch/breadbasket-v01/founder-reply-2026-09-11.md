Thelonious — all four landed. Short version: you found two bugs, one design gap, and one open question, and the okra was worse than it looked.

**The okra.** Two faults. When you say "deer ate them" you don't give a number, and the app was reading any claim without a number as *"he's still got okra and has just confirmed it"* — so instead of going, it became the freshest thing on your page. The rule now: a removal with no number means **all of it**; if it's only part, say the amount, and the card will show you what you'll be left with rather than what's coming off.

The second fault you didn't see. After you corrected "sold out" to "deer ate them", the app went back on its own a minute later and wrote the *sold out* version into your record nine times. Nothing you approved, nothing you saw. Those rows are being deleted and a lock added so any one card can only ever reach your record once. Nothing goes up unless you say so is the promise the whole thing rests on, so you should hear it from us rather than find it.

**Deer aren't spoilage — agreed.** A removal now records why: sold, spoiled, wildlife, pests, weather, donated, own use, in your words as well as ours. Customers never see the reason.

**The greyed block at the top** is an accident of ordering. Stale belongs below what's actually available, and will be.

**Varieties — yes, and it's in the design now.** A claim is about a crop and, where you name one, a variety. German Johnson and Brandywine under heirloom tomatoes; the four colours under bell peppers. Customers see one line per crop with the varieties named beneath it. And "the peppers are still good" will cover all four — you shouldn't have had to list them.

**Pushing now** (today/tomorrow): removals that actually remove, the lock that stops anything unapproved reaching your record, the nine bad okra rows cleaned out, the Sold out button fixed — it was quietly leaving crops on the page — and stale sorted below live. After that your page and your stock list will be true again without you re-entering anything.

**Next up:** crops with varieties under them, and merging the five tomato entries the rename created back into two. A week's work, not a day's.
