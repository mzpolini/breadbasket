# Loss records

*Briefing. Opened 12 September 2026, after he called this "a vital piece of data for farmers and extremely valuable."*

---

## What we're already holding

As of this week, every time a farmer takes something off his page, the system records **why**: sold, spoiled, wildlife, pests, weather, donated, own use — alongside his own sentence, in his own words. That came out of his okra: deer ate it, and the only word we had for it was "spoiled", which made his record claim food had rotted.

Two things fall out of that, and neither needs asking a farmer for anything extra:

- **We know what was on hand when it went.** A loss empties the position, and the ledger knows what the position was a moment earlier. "Ten pounds of red okra, lost to wildlife, 8 September" is already derivable from what he said, without anyone standing in a field doing arithmetic on a bad day.
- **We know what he said, verbatim.** Not a dropdown he picked — the sentence he spoke.

So a season of losses is being written down now, as a by-product of a farmer keeping his stand honest. Whatever we do with it later, the data starts accumulating from this week rather than from the day we decide to build something.

## What we don't know

**Who the record is actually for.** These want different things:

- *His own eye* — what a season cost him, what to plant more of, what to fence. Wants patterns and totals.
- *A disaster or assistance programme* — wants dates, acreage, cause, and evidence, in their format, by their deadline.
- *An insurer* — wants whatever the policy says, usually more than either.
- *A lender or a grant application* — wants a story about a bad year that stands up.

We can serve the first one well from what we hold today. The others we would be guessing at, and a form that is *nearly* what an agency wants is worse than no form, because a farmer finds out it was nearly right after the deadline.

**What a farmer is actually asked for.** We don't know which programmes his farmers use, whether they use them at all, or what they're asked to produce. That is knowable — some of it is published, some of it only an extension agent or a farmer who has filed a claim would know — and it changes what this is.

**Whether a record kept this way is trusted.** A note a farmer typed into an app in September may or may not count for anything in February. If it doesn't, this is still valuable to him privately, and we should say so honestly rather than imply a claim it can't support.

## What we'd want from him

1. **Have any of your farmers ever filed for a crop loss?** What did that look like — who did they talk to, what did they have to produce, and did anything come of it?
2. **Is there someone in that world we could ask?** An extension agent, a county office, a farmer who has been through it once. Ten minutes with someone who has filed is worth more than a week of us reading rules.
3. **Which of the four readers above is the one you care about?** If it's his own eye, this is close to free and we should just build it. If it's a programme, this is a research problem first and a build second.
4. **How much does a farmer already know about what he loses?** Is "the deer got a third of the okra this year" something he could tell you today, or genuinely news?
5. **Is there a version of this that helps him this season** — not a claim form, but knowing by October what August cost him?

## Where this stands

Not scheduled, deliberately. Recording continues either way, which buys the time to answer the above properly. The alternative — designing to a programme's requirements we have assumed rather than read — is how you build a form that nobody can file.
